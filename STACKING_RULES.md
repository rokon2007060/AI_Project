# Stacking Rules (Ludo-Style)

## Overview
The game now features a **stacking system** similar to Ludo, where players can create pairs and triples of their own pieces for defensive and offensive advantages.

## Key Rules

### 1. Creating Stacks
- **Move pieces onto friendly pieces** to create stacks
- Maximum stack size: **3 pieces**
- Stacks are indicated by a **number on the piece** (e.g., "2" for pair, "3" for triple)
- Single pieces show no number

### 2. Breaking Stacks
- You can move a piece from any stack at any time
- This **breaks the stack** and leaves remaining pieces behind
- Example: Moving 1 piece from a triple leaves a pair behind

### 3. Capture Rules (Stack Strength System)

#### Basic Principle
**Attacker stack size must be ≥ Defender stack size to capture**

#### Specific Rules:
- **Single piece (1):**
  - ✅ Can capture: Single pieces
  - ❌ Cannot capture: Pairs or triples
  
- **Pair (2 pieces):**
  - ✅ Can capture: Single pieces and pairs
  - ❌ Cannot capture: Triples
  
- **Triple (3 pieces):**
  - ✅ Can capture: Any stack (singles, pairs, or triples)
  - 💪 Most powerful configuration

#### Capture Behavior:
- When a stack is captured, the **entire stack is removed** from the board
- Capturing a triple with another triple removes all 6 pieces total!

## Strategic Implications

### Defensive Strategy
- **Create triples** in vulnerable positions for maximum protection
- **Triple = fortress** - only another triple can capture it
- Pairs provide moderate protection against singles

### Offensive Strategy
- **Build triples** to break through enemy defenses
- Use pairs to capture enemy pairs before they become triples
- Singles are vulnerable but mobile - use for quick maneuvers

### Risk vs Reward
- **Stacking Pros:**
  - Better defense
  - Can capture stronger enemy stacks
  - Control key board positions
  
- **Stacking Cons:**
  - Less board coverage
  - Fewer pieces to make moves with
  - Losing a triple means losing 3 pieces at once

## Visual Indicators

### On Board:
- **No number** = Single piece (1)
- **"2"** displayed on piece = Pair (2 pieces)
- **"3"** displayed on piece = Triple (3 pieces)

### Info Panel:
- Displays total piece count (including all stacked pieces)
- Example: "Red: 12" counts all red pieces, even if stacked

## Gameplay Tips

1. **Start defensive:** Create pairs near your back row
2. **Build triples** for critical board positions
3. **Attack with strength:** Use equal or greater stacks to capture
4. **Count carefully:** Losing a triple is a big setback
5. **Strategic unstacking:** Sometimes breaking a stack creates better tactical opportunities

## How It Compares to Traditional Checkers

| Traditional Checkers | New Stacking Rules |
|---------------------|-------------------|
| One piece per square | Up to 3 pieces per square |
| Any piece can capture any piece | Stack strength matters |
| Simple capture rules | Strategic depth with stacking |
| Focus on positioning | Focus on both positioning AND stack building |

## Examples

### Example 1: Failed Capture
- Red has a **single piece**
- White has a **pair** 
- ❌ Red cannot jump the white pair (insufficient stack strength)

### Example 2: Successful Capture
- Red has a **pair**
- White has a **single piece**
- ✅ Red can jump and capture the white single

### Example 3: Equal Strength
- Red has a **triple**
- White has a **triple**
- ✅ Either can capture the other (equal strength)
- ⚠️ Massive consequence: 6 pieces removed total!

## Game Balance

The stacking system adds:
- **Strategic depth:** Planning stack formations
- **Risk management:** When to stack vs spread pieces
- **Tactical choices:** Building defenses vs attacking
- **Resource management:** Protect your stacks, target enemy stacks

Enjoy the new strategic possibilities! 🎮
